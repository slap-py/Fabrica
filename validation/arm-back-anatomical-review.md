# Arm/back anatomical source review

Reviewed October 11, 2026. The datasets summarize typical anatomy for the named source components. Bilateral facts are authored once and expanded to distinct source IDs; no reflection or geometry edits occur. The unsuffixed `iliocostalis-colli-muscle` is the left source object. Shared shoulder entries use the same canonical information in Right Arm, Back and combined views.

The fact fields follow `src/muscleFacts.ts` and upper-leg `facts.ts`. Support fields follow upper-leg `supportFacts.ts`; each of 146 nerve/artery/vein objects has course and territory facts with linked sources. The two facts may use the same source. Source names/material grouping remain available in the manifest and inspector. The table labels do not establish source variant anatomy or complete mesh footprint boundaries.

## Evidence and differences resolved

- [UAMS upper-limb muscle table](https://medicine.uams.edu/neuroscience/education/medical-school-courses/human-structure-module/anatomy-tables/muscle-tables/muscles-of-the-upper-limb/) and [back table](https://medicine.uams.edu/neuroscience/education/medical-school-courses/human-structure-module/anatomy-tables/muscle-tables/muscles-of-the-back-region/) supply attachment, motor and vascular anatomy. Whole-muscle rows are subdivided by named heads or fiber portions; grouped interossei, lumbricals, digital flexors and rotatores remain grouped. Attachment text includes unmodeled hosts rather than being limited to clickable links.
- UAMS lists long radial wrist extensor under deep radial supply and gives an imprecise brevis origin at the supracondylar ridge. The [primary radial nerve dissection study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7345276/) supports longus supply proximal to the radial nerve division and variable brevis branching. Longus origin is the lateral supracondylar ridge; brevis uses the lateral epicondyle/common extensor tendon. Brevis branching variation is stated in its fact field.
- UAMS treats flexor pollicis brevis as one median-innervated row. The source has two heads. [Caetano et al., anatomical dissection study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5721304/) documents median/ulnar and dual supply. Superficial and deep heads have separate origins and qualified innervation; no variant is inferred from the mesh.
- Deep radial versus posterior interosseous terminology differs in the [UAMS nerve table](https://medicine.uams.edu/neuroscience/education/medical-school-courses/human-structure-module/anatomy-tables/nerve-tables/nerves-of-the-upper-limb/). Here posterior interosseous means the continuation after supinator. Cutaneous distribution is distinguished from articular sensory fibers.
- UAMS gives serratus posterior superior a rib 1–4 pattern. The dataset uses the commonly described rib 2–5 pattern, with T2–T5 intercostal supply. [The primary cadaver report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6093753/) demonstrates rib/level variation and discusses evidence against a definite respiratory role. Rib elevation/depression remains explicitly traditional; proprioception is proposed, not established. Inferior serratus uses ribs 9–12 as in UAMS; other reports extend to rib 8.
- [Back subdivision reference](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK537074/?report=printable) distinguishes iliocostalis, longissimus, spinalis, splenii and transversospinal muscles. Source `colli` is mapped to cervical/cervicis anatomy. Spinalis capitis/cervicis are variable and often poorly separated. The named source mesh is not evidence of a universal separate muscle.
- The UAMS intertransversarii row is too broad for the four lateral lumbar subdivision records. The [institutional lumbar anatomy thesis](https://documentserver.uhasselt.be/bitstream/1942/8767/1/Frank%20Vandenabeele.pdf) and [primary human dorsal-ramus study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7076562/) distinguish lateral intertransversarii from medial dorsal-ramus muscles. Both lateral dorsal/ventral parts receive ventral lumbar rami; the dorsal part links a transverse process to the accessory process above. This resolves the apparent conflict with generalized intrinsic-back dorsal innervation.
- [UAMS arteries](https://medicine.uams.edu/neuroscience/education/medical-school-courses/human-structure-module/anatomy-tables/artery-tables/arteries-of-the-upper-limb/) describe branch origins and territories. A source-named deep transverse cervical branch is preserved; independent dorsal scapular origin remains an explicitly stated variation. Deep/cervical, thoracic and lumbar muscle subdivisions link only to appropriate regional arterial families.
- [UAMS veins](https://medicine.uams.edu/neuroscience/education/medical-school-courses/human-structure-module/anatomy-tables/vein-tables/selected-veins-of-the-upper-limb/) covers independent superficial venous courses and the companion-vein principle. Median cubital/antebrachial termination and suprascapular termination are qualified where variable. Deep grouped veins do not assert branch-by-branch source correspondence.
- [OpenStax upper-limb bones](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/8-2-bones-of-the-upper-limb), [pectoral girdle](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/8-1-the-pectoral-girdle), [vertebral column](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/7-3-the-vertebral-column), [thoracic cage](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/7-4-the-thoracic-cage) and [selected joints](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/9-6-anatomy-of-selected-synovial-joints) support bone landmarks and actual articulations. The scapulothoracic interface is not recorded as a synovial joint; the distal ulna does not directly articulate with carpal bones. Ribs 11–12 lack costotransverse joints. Rib 10 facet anatomy varies; the graph uses its own-level articulation.
- [Wrist anatomy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK534779/), [primary wrist anatomical study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1233428/) and [Texas Tech hand guide](https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/musculoskeletal_system/hand_ans.html) support carpal/hand ligaments, retinacula and grouped connective tissue. Four source-band nomenclature mappings remain unresolved rather than being assigned guessed endpoints.
- [Thoracolumbar fascia anatomical review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3512278/) supports the three source layers; authors use different layer definitions. The anterior layer is described within the traditional three-layer account rather than claiming a single universal classification.

## Source taxonomy exceptions

The exported muscle count includes `flexor-retinaculum-of-wrist-r`, `extensor-retinaculum-of-wrist-r` and `superficial-transverse-metacarpal-ligament-r`. These are noncontractile connective tissue: their five structured fields explicitly state attachments/retaining function, no muscle motor supply, and unresolved fine sensory/vascular mapping. They retain their original tissue layer and canonical IDs. The [primary superficial transverse metacarpal study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41201689/) supports its web-space/fascial identity. The distal radioulnar disc likewise retains source ligament classification while its description identifies fibrocartilage.

## Attachment and clinical review

All 179 existing patch candidates are enumerated in `arm-back-information-coverage.json`: 38 previously accepted cards across 24 records agree with the written attachment end and same-side host. Latissimus origin reaches the sacrum indirectly through thoracolumbar fascia; its insertion remains the humerus, even though a source proximity candidate points at scapula. The 141 previously withheld candidates remain withheld. An anatomically plausible nearest host is insufficient to establish which written attachment end a source patch actually represents. No geometry, seeds or measured footprints were created or changed.

Selected clinical notes cover median nerve carpal-tunnel compression, axillary nerve vulnerability near the surgical neck and scaphoid proximal-fragment vascular risk. Their linked sources include the anatomical nerve tables and the [primary scaphoid vascularity study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7430591/). These are educational anatomical correlations, without treatment instructions.

## Review limits

Content review is by the implementing assistant against institutional tables, textbook chapters and anatomical studies; it is not independent anatomical peer review. Some references provide only abstracts or scanned historical material through the available reader. Those references remain background evidence; their presence does not mean every sentence was verified from full text. Small wrist-band nomenclature and source-specific segmentation remain qualified instead of being resolved by inference. Automated checks verify coverage, citation syntax, known regressions, relationship integrity and file preservation; they do not prove the truth of every anatomical statement.
